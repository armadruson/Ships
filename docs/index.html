07 — Method

Where the numbers come from

Source

All figures come from the annual lists of ships dismantled worldwide published by the NGO Shipbreaking Platform. Each list names every large ocean-going commercial ship broken that year, with its IMO number, type, size, age, flag, owners and scrapping yard. The Platform compiles them from several sources and cross-checks them. Coverage starts with the 2012 list. Last Voyage is an independent visualisation and is not affiliated with the Platform.

How it stays current

A scheduled GitHub Action checks the Platform's list page every week. When a new or corrected list appears, it downloads the file, re-runs the pipeline and republishes this site. New annual lists usually appear in January or February. Nobody has to press a button.

Definitions

Year
The year of the list a ship appears in, which is roughly the year it arrived at the breakers.
South Asia
Ships broken in India, Bangladesh or Pakistan. Nearly all of them are beached at Alang, Chattogram or Gadani.
Ship type
The source lists more than 300 type labels. We group them into eight families by keyword.
Age
List year minus year built.
Gross tonnage
Reported from the 2014 list onwards only, so tonnage charts start in 2014.

What we left out, on purpose

Scrap prices ($/LDT) appear only in the 2012–13 lists. Weekly broker price reports exist, but they only go back to about 2021 and their publishers restrict republication. Light displacement tonnage has large gaps. Metrics we can't track consistently for the whole period aren't shown.
